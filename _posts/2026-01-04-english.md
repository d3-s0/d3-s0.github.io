English language is the most important programming language. English language underpins much of modern western society. Which is why it is imperative you understand it well. Without building your toolkit understanding of the language you are a captain of a ship without a rudder to take you to get you where you want to. Most people have a rudimentary understanding of the language. The nuances of how it works. We were taught some terms briefly in school only to forget them soon after.  

What makes up a language? It is the grammar, the words and the types of writing. Grammar is the structure.  The words the meaning. The types are fiction and non-fiction with subdivisions within it.

## Grammar
Grammar is the structure of a language - not what words mean, but the way the words fit together. It’s the architect from which you build the sentences - once you have the frame ready you can write anything. 
- Noun:  person, place or thing
- Verb: action, state of being, event
- Adverb: modifies or describes verb
- Adjective: describes noun
- Preposition: words that show direction, location or time
- Clause = subject + verb
- Independent clause = can stand alone
- Dependent clause 
- Compound sentence= independent clause + coordinating conjunction  + independent  clause
- Complex sentence
- Complex compound sentence = 2 or more independent  clauses and one dependent clause
- Conjunction: connects clauses or sentences 
- Pronouns: takes place of a person, place or thing
Active an passive voice 
- Verb can be active(subject does something) or passive
- Usually active voice is better. Make sure it’s consistent.
- Moods of verbs
    - Indicative verbs
    - Imperative verbs
    - Interrogative verbs
    - conditional verbs: used to express things that would happen, if circumstances  were right
    - Subjunctive verbs
Affixes and roots

We are always communicating.  The best communicators are great storytellers. Humans are hardwired to love a good story. It connects our rational and emotional sides of our brain and it sticks with us. Stories allow you to experience multiple lifetimes from different people’s perspective. Stories can be broken into:
- Plot
- Characters
- World building

## Plot – Set up
- Balance – the world in balance.
- Disruption – introduce event that throws things out of balance

### World building
- Geography: Continents, oceans, weather, and maps.
- Social: Governments, laws, money, and power structures.
- Culture and History: Religions, holidays, clothing, and past wars.
- Daily Life: Food, technology, architecture, and language.
- Magic laws: Explicit boundaries for what is scientifically or mystically possible

### Characters
Characters drive the plot by making choices and feeling emotions. They let us experience different lives and understand other people. Characters take action, face problems, learn lessons and show feelings. We feel close to the characters, we want to learn the truth of people, and curious how they fix hard problems. We also love magic.

## Plot - Conflict
- Facing obstacle: Main character faces the obstacles/conflict to restore balance
- Vulnerability: main character exposes internal fears, obstacles, mistakes
- What does my protagonist want in order to restore balance in his or her life? 
- What is keeping my protagonist from achieving his or her desire? 
- How would my protagonist decide to act in order to achieve his or her desire in the face of those antagonistic forces?
- What internal clash in logic does character experience? 

## Plot - Resolution
- The Transformation: Reveal the outcome of the climax and how you changed.
- The New Normal: Show your updated reality and how it differs from the beginning.


Objective summary: paraphrased version of text without personal opinion or judgment. Focus on: facts, what is important, in order, check facts.
Textual analysis: information in text that backs up your analysis
	- explicit: plainly stated in text
	- implicit: evidence that is only implied by what is stated in the text. When we make a guess based on implicit or explicit evidence. We’re drawing an inference.
Best evidence is evidence that everyone would agree this evidence leads to the same conclusion. 


## Authorship and Narration 
Every author has a different background and experience. This includes culture, time period, location and personal experience. 
Narrator is the person who tells the story. The author creates the narrator. Think is the narrator reliable? Do they hide the truth? 

## World building
Setting is the surroundings and time in which the events of a story take place.
The setting tells us the period, location, weather, surroundings of character and social characteristics. 
The author sets the scene: what you might see, hear, feel smell or taste. What big events have just happened. Where? When? What are the conditions? 

## Character
Character is an individual in a story. 
Types of characters: main character, protagonist, antagonist 
Flat character: can be summarised in a few lines
Round character: lots of depth and detail characters
Sympathetic characters: characters we can relate to 
Dynamic character: changes during the course of the story
Narrator: can be a character in the story
Point of view
- First person narrator: 
- Second person: as it is happening to you. 
- Third person: character is not in the story - refers to the characters as “he” or “she”. Feels omniscient, which means the narrator knows all. 
Not all characters mean what they say, look for what they do. What they do might contradict their words and vice versa.
