We humans are incredibly confusing. This is my attempt to understand us.
What motivates humans?
**Maslow's hierachy of needs**
At our base the main motivations are based on Maslow's hierachy of needs:
At our base, every human is trying to climb Maslow's pyramid. However, Honor Culture and Dignity Culture provide completely different toolkits (software) to help the brain (hardware) satisfy those needs.
1. Self actualisation: desire to become the most one can be
2. Esteem: respect, self-esteem, freedom
3. Love and belonging: friendship, family
4. Safety needs: personal security, employment, resources, health, property
5. Physiological needs: air, water, food, shelter, sleep, clothing


**Shalom Schwartz’s Theory of Basic Human Values**
- Self-Direction: Independent thought and action, choosing, creating, and exploring.
- Stimulation: Excitement, novelty, and challenge in life.
- Hedonism: Pleasure and sensuous gratification for oneself.
- Achievement: Personal success through demonstrating competence according to social standards.
- Power: Social status, prestige, and control or dominance over people and resources.
- Security: Safety, harmony, and stability of society, relationships, and self.
- Conformity: Restriction of actions and impulses that likely upset or violate social norms.
- Tradition: Respect, commitment, and acceptance of the customs and ideas that traditional culture or religion provide.
- Benevolence: Preserving and enhancing the welfare of people with whom one is in frequent personal contact.
- Universalism: Understanding, appreciation, tolerance, and protection for the welfare of all people and nature

**More values**
- Connection: Family, deep friendships, community, empathy, love.
- Growth: Curiosity, competence, health, resilience, ambition.
- Freedom: Autonomy, adventure, creativity, financial independence.
- Integrity: You speak the truth, even when a lie would save you from awkwardness or 
conflict.
- Honor: I refuse to be privy to what I deem dishonorable speech. If someone makes a bigoted, disloyal, or scurrilous remark, I will get up and leave the table.
- Justice: I treat the weak and the powerful with the exact same measure of respect. I will not flatter the high, nor oppress the low.
- Resolution – Once my course is set by right principle, I will not waver. I mean what I say, I finish what I start, and I do not let convenience dictate my commitment.
- Courage – I will speak the truth and do what is right, even when I stand entirely alone. Fear will not negotiate my principles.
- Discipline – I do what needs to be done, when it needs to be done, whether I feel like it or not. I am the master of my impulses, not their slave.


**human culture**
If the brain is the hardware, culture is the software we write together to keep that hardware from crashing.  human culture is cumulative, it never resets to zero; it only builds.

Culture has been our prime survival technique rather than biological evolution - through social learning and shared systems. Culture can be split into two main categories..
- Material Culture (Physical Adaptation)    
    - houses
    - wealth
    - clothes
- Immaterial Culture (Social Cohesion and Order)
    - law
    - politics
    - religion
    - class and caste systems
    - education
    - sports teams/ media consumption

Once we feel part of the group, we tend to feel automatic hostility towards outsiders.
Tribal thinking was needed when fighting for survival of scarce resources but the modern world is different. Our world has plenty of resources to sustain a happy existence. But what do we deep a happy existence?

If you ask someone from the western society, it is highly likely they will value individual freedom and personal success. However, eastern cultures place a  higher value on family duty, and respect for authority. 
We tend to have conflicts when one society tries to force its values onto a culture that operates on a completely different value set. People are programming from a young age. We are pulled and pushed to believe different things. A culture you are born into dictates what you consider right/wrong


**What made us different**
Our:
    - Brain: High frontal-lobe connectivity; optimised for abstract thought and speech.
    - Specific Comms: Fully flexible, syntax-based language. Can discuss past, future, and invisible concepts
    - Knowledge accumulation: we passed on knowledge.
    - Social group size: Unlimited group sizes bound together by shared myths and culture (Dunbar's Number ~150 baseline, scaled to millions).

So what happened was:
1. The Brain gave us the raw computational power and abstract wiring.
2. Flexible Language allowed us to output those abstract thoughts into the physical world.
3. Knowledge Accumulation meant our thoughts didn't die with us; they accumulated across generations.
4. Unlimited Group Sizes meant millions of brains could simultaneously network together using that accumulated knowledge.

Because of this, Homo sapiens became the only species on Earth to escape the limits of biological evolution.

our brains take so long to mature, the cultural narratives we absorb between ages 0 and 25 become our biological default settings. If your culture tells you that your "class, caste, or gender" dictates your ceiling, your prefrontal cortex actually wires itself to prune away alternative futures. The "invisible hand" is real because it is made of neural pathways forged during childhood

## the brain

Is it our brains? Our brains are what gives us the ability 
to become the dominant species on earth. The brain can be split into:
- The **reptilian brain** controls survival (breathing, heart rate). 
- The **limbic system** manages **emotions and memory**. 
- The **neocortex** handles **logic, language, and abstract thought**.
The part that makes us special is the limbic and neocortex parts. Humans have an unprecedented biological "cross-talk" between the two that creates unique superpowers.

**The Frontal-Lobe Hyper-Connectivity (Our Internal Router)**
-  In a monkey, the emotional center (limbic system) triggers an immediate reaction. In a human, that emotional signal has to pass through a massive prefrontal "router." This allows us to pause, simulate the future, suppress impulsive urges, and override basic instincts based on social rules or long-term goals.


**Micro-Architecture: Von Economo Neurons (The Social Circuit)**
VENs are long, fast-acting cells. They act like an express train, allowing our brain to instantly process complex social cues (like a slight change in a friend's facial expression or tone of voice) and connect it to our own emotional state. This is the biological foundation for your fourth pillar: maintaining massive social groups.

**Neoteny: The Brain That Refuses to Grow Up**
A human baby is born biologically premature because its giant head wouldn't otherwise fit through the birth canal. At birth, our brains are only about 25% of their adult size. It takes nearly 25 years for a human prefrontal cortex to fully mature. This allows culture to program the hardware.

## culture 

An honor culture where if you insult them the conflict can keep going even if logic can resolve it quickly. To those members, public honor is important and if not restored they will see that others think of them as weak. It is a zero sum game, where insults are done to improve one's standing at the cost of another. These types of cultures can go for many generations as logic and reason cannot resolve them due to the subjective nature of honor. The other form of culture is dignity culture, where members are taught that words cannot affect you, instead conflict is channelled through legal bodies(law, HR, police) to achieve retribution. Members will need a strong self-worth value to do this.

The culture you are born into often also decides your access to the cultural areas you want to go to, and even how you see your own potential. This invisble hand guides most of us through life.




## adult devleopment
Robert keegan's theory of adult development tries to set out the stages of most human development:
- Stage 1: Impulsive Mind (Early Childhood): Driven by impulses and perceptions.
- Stage 2: Imperial Mind (Adolescence): Ruled by personal needs, wishes, and interests; and driven by "self-sovereign" perspectives.
- Stage 3: Socialised Mind: wants to be part of the tribe. Able to think abstractly and of others.
- Stage 4: Self-Authoring Minds: Individuals can step back from social expectations to create their own internal authority, values, and sense of identity.
- Stage 5: Self-Transforming Mind: Individuals can hold multiple, conflicting systems of meaning, recognising the limitations of their own perspectives

Most of society stays in socialised mind stage for most of their lives. However, we should strive to bein stage 4, self-authoring. How do we do this? Instead of thinking "What does the culture expect of me?" Ask "What are my core values, and does this action align with them?" How do we make our core principles in life (e.g., freedom, justice, kindness, adventure, self-worth)? Once we have them, we can measure the options against the values, not against what society, your peers, or your family will think.

Humans move through biological and psychological transitions. Erik Erikson’s Stages of Psychosocial Development breaks this down by core existential conflicts:
- Adolescence: Identity vs. Role Confusion (Who am I, and where do I fit?)
- Early Adulthood: Intimacy vs. Isolation (Can I love and be loved?)
- Middle Adulthood: Generativity vs. Stagnation (How can I contribute to the next generation?)
- Late Adulthood: Ego Integrity vs. Despair (Did my life have meaning?)

## how to communicate

humans are deeply emotional, narrative-driven creatures. Because humans are a mix of limbic emotion and neocortex logic, communication is rarely just about exchanging data.
- The Information Level (Neocortex - logic): The literal words, facts, and logic being used.
- The Relational Level (Limbic - emotion): The subtext. "Do you respect me? Am I safe with you? Are we in the same tribe?"

**step 1: mindset**
Adjust your internal settings.  Communication fails when it comes from a place of judgment or ego.
- Positivity and Respect: Humans naturally move toward warmth and away from hostility. To build trust, you must treat others the way they want to be treated (The Platinum Rule).
- The 2:1 Ratio: Humans possess two ears and one mouth for a biological reason—you should listen twice as much as you speak.
- Curiosity Over Judged Outcomes: View different points of view not as threats, but as tools to increase your personal creativity and make more informed decisions. When you test your own assumptions against someone else's, you develop true clarity on your own positions.

**step 2: delivery (Neocortex)**
To ensure the literal message is received clearly, a human must control their physiological delivery:
- Enunciate and Pace: Speak slowly enough to be understood and concentrate on your consonants so your words do not slur together.
- Conceptual Filtering: Know exactly what you want to say before you open your mouth. Avoid overloading your message with too many points.
- Adaptability: Match the receiver's preference. 
    - Some humans need fine details, while others need big concepts. 
    - Some need silence to organize thoughts, while others talk to think. 
    - Match their channel (written, phone, or face-to-face).
- The Five-Gate Filter: Speak only if your words meet five strict criteria: Accurate, Appropriate, Effective, Necessary, and Timely.
- Force yourself to speak and write with extreme clarity and precision.

**step 3: active listening (Limbic)**
As the quote notes, "Listening is the willingness to change." It signals safety to the other person's limbic system.
- Zero Interruptions: Let others finish making their points entirely before interjecting yours.
- The Three-Step Echo: Confirm understanding and show you are genuinely interested by using these three tools:
	- Paraphrase: Restate the factual data they just said in your own words.
	- Reflect: Mirror the underlying emotion ("It sounds like you felt incredibly frustrated by that").
	- Summarise: Wrap up their main ideas and feelings together before you take your turn to speak.

**Resolving Conflicts/Aligning narratives**
conflict does not destroy relationships; it clears the air and revitalizes them
1. Reflect Understanding: Start by proving you heard them. “What I am hearing is that the timeline feels unrealistic for your team.”
2. Validate and Respect: Acknowledge their right to their worldview without necessarily agreeing with the conclusion. “I understand why you feel anxious about this, and I respect your commitment to quality.”
3. State Your Position: Share your perspective by anchoring it in the relationship rather than a desire to win. “My goal is to make sure we hit our launch date together, not to cause unnecessary stress.”
4. Separate Behavior from Identity: Praise frequently. If you must criticize, focus strictly on the behavior and never the person. Never insult, disparage, or put down their character.

Example:
1. Step 1: Reflect Understanding
Sarah: "Mark, I wanted to chat about the new tracking system. What I am hearing is that migrating to this software feels like an unnecessary administrative burden that slows down your actual engineering work. Is that accurate?"
Mark: "Yes! It takes me twice as long to log a task, and it's distracting."
2. Step 2: Validate & Respect
Sarah: "I understand why you feel frustrated by this change. You have a system that has successfully delivered projects here for five years, and I deeply respect how reliable your output is." (Validating his need for Tradition/Security without agreeing that the software is bad).
3. Step 3: State Your Position (Anchored in the Relationship)
Sarah: "My goal here isn't to micromanage you or create busywork. I value how smoothly your team operates. The reason I'm pushing for this is that upper management is cutting budgets based on untracked data. I want to use this data to protect our team's resources and prove how hard you all work." (Aligning their narratives so they are in the same "tribe").
4. Step 4: Focus on Behavior & Find a Solution
Sarah: "Let’s look at the specific behavior causing the issue. If the interface is too clunky, let's customize your dashboard together so it only takes you two minutes a day. Would you be open to trying that for one week?"
Mark: "If it only takes two minutes and protects our budget... yeah, I can try that."

**Facing hard situations**
Affective Labeling (The 3-Second Pause): When you experience an intense emotional reaction—like anger, anxiety, or the urge to procrastinate—your limbic system is attempting a hijack. Force a 3-second pause and verbally label the emotion: "I am feeling anxious about this deadline." Neurological studies show that simply naming an emotion shifts brain activity from the emotional amygdala straight to the logical prefrontal cortex.

- Action: When you feel criticized, insulted, or rejected, implement a mandatory 10-second pause before responding. Breathe and mentally label the feeling: "This is a limbic spike. It is a false survival alarm."
- Why: This physically forces the emotional signal to travel through your high-speed prefrontal white matter highways, shifting your response from an impulsive reaction to a logical, calculated decision.

- Action: When resolving conflicts at work or in life, never back an emotional person into a corner. Always provide them with a clear, polite, and logical exit that lets them preserve their public honor.
- Why: Most people operate on raw Honor templates under stress. If you threaten their standing, they will fight irrationally. If you protect their ego, you win the practical outcome.
