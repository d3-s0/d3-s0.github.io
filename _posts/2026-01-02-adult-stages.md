Humans are incredibly confusing. Robert Keegan's theory of adult development tries to set out the stages of most human development:
- Stage 1: Impulsive Mind (Early Childhood): Driven by impulses and perceptions.
- Stage 2: Imperial Mind (Adolescence): Ruled by personal needs, wishes, and interests; and driven by "self-sovereign" perspectives.
- Stage 3: Socialised Mind: wants to be part of the tribe. Able to think abstractly and of others.
- Stage 4: Self-Authoring Minds: Individuals can step back from social expectations to create their own internal authority, values, and sense of identity.
- Stage 5: Self-Transforming Mind: Individuals can hold multiple, conflicting systems of meaning, recognising the limitations of their own perspectives

Most of society stays in socialised mind stage for most of their lives. However, we should strive to being stage 4, self-authoring. How do we do this? Instead of thinking "What does the culture expect of me?" Ask "What are my core values, and does this action align with them?" How do we make our core principles in life (e.g., freedom, justice, kindness, adventure, self-worth)? Once we have them, we can measure the options against the values, not against what society, your peers, or your family will think.

But what motivates us? We can look at the Maslow's hierarchy of needs which ranks:
1. self-actualisation: desire to become the most one can be
2. esteem: respect, recognition, status
3. love and belonging: friendship, intimacy, family
4. safety needs: security, employment, health, property
5. physiological needs: air, water, food, shelter, sleep
