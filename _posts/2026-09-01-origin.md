# The Big Bang Theory
How did the universe start? The current scientific theory is the Big Bang theory. The theory states that the universe started approximately 13.8 billion years ago - including space and time. In that moment all matter was concentrated in a single dense point. Then an explosion caused all matter to expand rapidly from this point (and it's still expanding!). How do we know this? We have three pieces of evidence:
- Cosmic microwave background radiation: cooled remnant afterglow of the Big Bang.
- Hubble’s law: galaxies recede from each other.
- Light element abundance. The universe started with only light elements (mostly hydrogen 75% and helium 25%) then nuclear fusion in stars formed heavier elements.

What happened before the big bang? No one knows!

As matter moved through the universe, collisions caused matter to coalesce and form stars.  Young stars use hydrogen and helium and release energy through nuclear fusion, held together by their own gravity. Our Sun is a star that formed about 9 billion years ago and is situated in a galaxy called the Milky Way.  To give a sense of scale, the Milky Way is just one of approximately 400 billion galaxies in the observable universe.

# The Earth

The Earth, formed 4.6 billion years ago with dust surrounding the Sun. Then 4.5 billion years ago a Mars sized body collided with Earth, resulting in debris that eventually formed the Moon. Earth was a molten mass, constantly bombarded by space matter where it cooled and formed a thin crust. Water vapor condensed, forming Earth's oceans.  Approx 3.8 billion years ago, first life appeared on Earth. 
Microorganisms likely formed in hydrothermal vents or hot springs.


Then over many years of slow evolution Dinosaurs and mammals emerged about 230 millions years ago.  The supercontinent Pangea began breaking up into the North America & Eurasia (Europe/Asia) and South America, Africa, India, Antarctica, & Australia. Then 66 million years ago, an astroid impact led to end of the age of dinosaurs. 7 million years ago, the first hominins emerged with key features being bipedalism and smaller canine teeth. 
Then 300,000 years ago, the first Homo sapiens appeared in Africa—that's us our key evolved parts being larger brains and opposable thumbs!

# Human migration
100,000 years ago, Homo sapiens began migrating out of Africa. We started using fire, forming shelters, and hunting with weapons. From 10,000 to 5,000 years ago, Humans went from nomadic cultures to settled agriculture. Civilisation grew up independently in Mesopotamia, Egypt, Indus Valley, China, Mesoamerica, and the Andes.

# The End of humanity
All good things must come to an End and so will humanity. The question is when and how? The two main causes are by nature and man:

### Nature caused:
- Asteroid impacts (like 66 million years ago when an astroid wiped out the dinosaurs)  
- Sun becomes a red giant: the Sun will engulf Mercury and Venus. Earth may survive but become uninhabitable due to extreme heat.

### Man caused:
- Nuclear war - Currently the world is in a mexican stand off, where if one sets of a nuclear bomb so will the others. The danger is if we have one nuke trigger friendly president it’s game over.

- Climate change - while it's unlikely to cause the end of humanity. It likely will be a cause for the end of modern civilisation. The luxuries we are used to be strained and humanity will have dark periods.

- Engineered pandemics - another COVID like virus could potentially wipe out a large chunk of humanity if we can't find a vaccine.

- Cyberattacks - security systems could be hacked by rapidly changing technologies like AI causing destabilisation of modern digital infrastructure.
