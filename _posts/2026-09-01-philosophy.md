We humans are incredibly confusing. This is my attempt to understand us.
What makes us, us? Is it our brains? Our brains are what gives us the ability 
to become the dominant species on earth. The brain can be split into:
- The reptilian brain controls survival (breathing, heart rate). 
- The limbic system manages emotions and memory. 
- The neocortex handles logic, language, and abstract thought.
The part that makes us special is the limbic and neocortex parts.

As we interact with our human units we use these complex parts that can handle 
emotions and decision making to form larger units. We fear rejection of the group 
since that means not surviving. Culture is our prime survival technique. Our culture
to build houses, clothes, laws, religions, belief systems, sports teams, media consumption, class structures, wealth, education, political parties. Once we feel part of the group, we tend to feel automatic hostility towards outsiders.
Tribal thinking was needed when fighting for survival of scarce resources but the modern world is different.
Our world has plenty of resources to sustain a happy existence. But what do we deep a happy existence?
If you ask someone from the western society, it is highly likely they will value individual freedom and personal success. However, eastern cultures place a  higher value on family duty, and respect for authority. 
We tend to have conflicts when one society tries to force its values onto a culture that operates on a completely different value set. People are programming from a young age. We are pulled and pushed to believe different things. A culture you are born into dictates what you consider right/wrong, how you see time (linear or circular) and what you value. 

An honor culture where if you insult them the conflict can keep going even if logic can resolve it quickly. To those members, public honor is important and if not restored they will see that others think of them as weak. It is a zero sum game, where insults are done to improve one's standing at the cost of another. These types of cultures can go for many generations as logic and reason cannot resolve them due to the subjective nature of honor. The other form of culture is dignity culture, where members are taught that words cannot affect you, instead conflict is channelled through legal bodies(law, HR, police) to achieve retribution. Members will need a strong self-worth value to do this.

The culture you are born into often also decides your access to the cultural areas you want to go to, and even how you see your own potential. This invisble hand guides most of us through life.

Robert keegan's theory of adult development tries to set out the stages of most human development:
- Stage 1: Impulsive Mind (Early Childhood): Driven by impulses and perceptions.
- Stage 2: Imperial Mind (Adolescence): Ruled by personal needs, wishes, and interests; and driven by "self-sovereign" perspectives.
- Stage 3: Socialised Mind: wants to be part of the tribe. Able to think abstractly and of others.
- Stage 4: Self-Authoring Minds: Individuals can step back from social expectations to create their own internal authority, values, and sense of identity.
- Stage 5: Self-Transforming Mind: Individuals can hold multiple, conflicting systems of meaning, recognising the limitations of their own perspectives

Most of society stays in socialised mind stage for most of their lives. However, we should strive to bein stage 4, self-authoring. How do we do this? Instead of thinking "What does the culture expect of me?" Ask "What are my core values, and does this action align with them?" How do we make our core principles in life (e.g., freedom, justice, kindness, adventure, self-worth)? Once we have them, we can measure the options against the values, not against what society, your peers, or your family will think.

# Thinking for yourself
How do you think? Give your own brain a chance to formulate an opinion. 
Do not crowd sourcing your identity. Write the First Draft in the Dark: Write your first draft completely from memory and intuition. Do not use quotes, citations, or references. This forces your unique voice, vocabulary, and structural logic to the surface. 

- Implement a "Delayed Search" Rule
- Consume High Quality Source Material
- The "Agree, Disagree, Pivot" Method: Every time you bring in an outside thought, you must actively position yourself against it. 
    - Do you agree? If so, what unique nuance did the author miss that you can add?
    - Do you disagree? Where is the flaw in their logic or data?
    - Can you pivot? How does their point apply to a completely different context?

- Only during the editing phase should you look up facts or external theories to anchor or challenge your ideas.
Self-authoring in writing means you stop treating other thinkers as authorities and start treating them as peers you are having a conversation with.

When we use our brains to make decisions in new settings we are learning.

Blooms taxonomy teaches the levels of learning, where creating is the highest form. Does that mean writing new content in your own words the best form of learning something new. No - It needs to go further. You need to combine that new idea with other ideas to generate/build something entirely original/novel.
How do you do this? Here are some techniques to practice when you learn something new:
- **The Cross Pollination Method**: Take the new concept you just learned (Concept A) and force it to interact with a completely different hobby, industry, or concept you already know well (Concept B). E.g.:
    - Concept A (Just learned): Bloom's Taxonomy.
    - Concept B (Existing knowledge): Video game design.
    - The New Creation: Designing a school curriculum where students "level up" and unlock achievements based on Bloom's tiers, turning a history class into an RPG (Role-Playing Game).
- **The SCAMPER Framework**:
    - Substitute: What components or rules can I swap out?
    - Combine: Can I merge this with another tool or process?
    - Adapt: How can this be tweaked to work in a completely different context?
    - Modify/Magnify: What happens if I make one part of this massive or tiny?
    - Put to another use: How would an artist, a scientist, or a chef use this?
    - Eliminate: What is the most complex part of this? What happens if I completely remove it?
    - Reverse: What if I flip the order or do the exact opposite of what is expected?
- **The "Yes, And..."** Improvisation Rule: When you read a piece of information, do not just summarise it. Force yourself to extend it.
    - Step 1 (Summary): "The author says that remote work increases employee happiness."
    - Step 2 (The Extension): "Yes, and because happiness rises, companies will likely see a massive drop in turnover, which means HR budgets will shift away from hiring and toward long-term employee development programs."



# Communication
Humans are uniquely cooperative, and communication is the invisible glue that holds our societies together.
- Beyond Words: Only a fraction of our communication is verbal. We rely heavily on non-verbal cues (body language, micro-expressions) and paralanguage (tone, pitch, pacing) to determine if someone is trustworthy.
- Shared Intentionality: Humans have a unique ability to collaborate toward a common goal because we can understand what another person is thinking and feeling (Theory of Mind). We don't just communicate data; we communicate shared realities.Minding your attitude
- focussed expectation, let go of judegements, blame or negative thoughts. You are now ready for positivity. People are drawn to positive attitudes. 

Engaging with your eyes
- eye contact indicates confidence, trust
Know what you want to say before you open your mouth, then make sure you can be understood.
- concentrate on your consonants to make sure you communicate what you mean
Whajado today?""What did you do today?"The tongue taps the roof of the mouth sharply for each D."I wanna go to the store.""I want to go to the store."The crisp NT and T provide rhythmic punctuation."Gimme a sec.""Give me a second."The biting V and final ND blend prevent slurring.

- highlight key words
- speak slowly enough to be understood
- lacing your sentences with appropriate words 
- avoid overloading your message with too many point
Listening with willingness
- 'Listening is the willingness to change'
- Powerful people need to be put in environments where they aren't as powerful to change

Letting go of negatvitiy


Feeding back what you heard
1. Paraphrase what you hear
2. Reflect the feeling behind the message
3. Summarise the speaker's main ideas and feelings to show understanding


Treat the other person with respect, politeness and kindness. How do you do this?
- encourage people to express their ideas and opinions
- letting others finish making their points before interjecting yours
- never insult, disparage or put down people or their points of view
- praise more frequently than you critise, and when you do criticise, focusing o the behaviour and not the person
- treat people the way they want to be treated

Preferred form of communication
- some people like to talk while they think and others prefer silence as they organise their thoughts
- some people like to focus on fine details and practical applications, others prefer concepts
- some people like the written word while others prefer phone or face to face

Thinking before you speak
- speak only if your words are: accurate, appropriate, effective, necessary, timely

Talking less and listening more
- 2 ears and 1 mouth
- confirm understanding by rephrasing what soemone says, and avoid interuptions.

Asking questions to understand
- be interested in what they say
- ask questions to gain information - the more you ask the more you find out

Handling disagreements with diplomacy
- come from different points of view
- handled well, conflict can clear the air and revitalise a relationship
- how to fix?
    - reflect your understanding of the other person's view or opinion. What I am hearing is ...
    - you respect the other person's opinion: I understand how you feel...
    - State your position or opinion - you value their relationship not creating conflict

Opening yourself to new ideas
You have been raised with a set of beliefs and values. The same is true for others. That doesn't mean you are right and they're wrong; you're just different. Open yourself to other points of view:
- make more informed decisions
- increase your personal creativity
- develop certainty, clarity on your own positions

Unconsidered idea lacks justification. When you consider new ides you may discover solid reasons for believing in what you do. Think from another point of view.
No one likes people who refuse to listen.
