# Astronomy
Astronomy is studying space using observation and theory. The first physicists were astronomers looking at the stars. The Egyptians and Babylonians. Constellations were mapped by Babylonians around 1900BCE. The Greeks then took it and adapted it. The Mayans developed calendars to calculate orbital periods and year lengths more precisely than Europeans. The sky was mapped by them in the study of astrology – this didn’t die until 1600s.  Geo-centrism (earth is at the center) was the common conception at the time. Copernicus put forward Heliocentrism that sun is at the centre. Galileo arrested for supporting heliocentrism.

# Classical mechanics
Mechanics describes motion of objects and forces that act on them. Sir Isaac Newton (1642–1727) was a central figure in pulling together the work of predecessors like Kepler and Galileo into a cohesive framework of classical mechanics. His 1687 Principia established the three laws of motion and universal gravitation, fundamentally changing how the universe was understood.

# Magnetism
Magnetism is a physical force made by moving electrical charges that allow them to attract or repel each other. If you wave a magnet near a coil of wire, the changing magnetic field forces the electrons in the wire to move, creating a voltage

#### Magnetic Forces & Motion
1. Force on a current: F=BIl
2. Force on a moving charge: F=qvB
3. The Right-Hand Rule: Formulas tell you how strong a force is, but this visual rule tells you which way the force will physically push the wire or charge.

#### Magnetic Flux & Induction
1. Magnetic flux: ϕ=BA
2. Magnetic flux linkage: Nϕ=BANcos(θ)
3. Faraday's Law of Induction
4. Lenz's Law (The Negative Sign): The induced current will always flow in a direction that creates a magnetic field opposing the change that created it.

# Thermodynamics
Thermodynamics is the study of heat, work, and energy. 

It explains how energy moves and changes forms.  System & Surroundings: The system is the specific object or space you are studying (like a piston full of gas, or a boiling kettle). The surroundings are everything else outside of it.
Heat (Q): Measured in Joules (J). This is thermal energy transferring between things due to a temperature difference. Heat always flows naturally from hot to cold. Work (W): Measured in Joules (J). This is mechanical energy transferred when a force moves an object. In thermodynamics, it usually means a gas expanding and pushing a piston. Internal Energy (U): Measured in Joules (J). This is the total hidden energy stored inside a substance—essentially the kinetic energy of all its microscopically vibrating atoms
Energy to change temperature

Before looking at energy transfer, we need to know how gases behave. The Ideal Gas Law links the pressure, volume, and temperature of a trapped gas.

Energy cannot be created or destroyed, only transferred. The change in internal energy of a system equals the heat added to the system minus the work done by the system.

# Relativity
#### Special relativity
Special relativity is the rule that the speed of light never changes, which means that space and time must stretch or shrink depending on how fast you are moving

#### General relativity (mass and gravity)
General relativity is the rule that mass and energy warp space and time, which is what creates the effect we feel as gravity.

Instead of gravity being an invisible pulling force, imagine space and time as a giant trampoline. A heavy object like the Earth makes a dip in the fabric, and smaller objects like the Moon or a falling apple just roll down that dip.

# Quantum physics
Quantum physics is the study of the universe at the smallest possible scale—atoms, electrons, and photons. At this level, nature stops acting like predictable, continuous billard balls and starts acting like fuzzy, overlapping waves of probability.

Quantisation: Energy is not a smooth ramp; it is a staircase. Matter and light can only absorb or release energy in tiny, discrete packets called quanta.

Wave-Particle Duality: Everything travels like a wave but interacts like a particle. An electron can ripple through space like water, but hits a detector at one precise point.
