## Astronomy
The first physicists were astronomers looking at the stars. The Egyptians and Babylonians. Constellations were mapped by Babylonians around 1900BCE. The Greeks then took it and adapted it. The Mayans developed calendars to calculate orbital periods and year lengths more precisely than Europeans. The sky was mapped by them in the study of astrology – this didn’t die until 1600s.  Geo-centrism (earth is at the center) was the common conception at the time.  Copernicus put forward Heliocentrism that sun is at the centre. Galileo arrested for supporting heliocentrism.

# Thermodynamics
Thermodynamics is the study of heat, work, and energy. It explains how energy moves and changes forms.  System & Surroundings: The system is the specific object or space you are studying (like a piston full of gas, or a boiling kettle). The surroundings are everything else outside of it.
Heat (Q): Measured in Joules (J). This is thermal energy transferring between things due to a temperature difference. Heat always flows naturally from hot to cold. Work (W): Measured in Joules (J). This is mechanical energy transferred when a force moves an object. In thermodynamics, it usually means a gas expanding and pushing a piston. Internal Energy (U): Measured in Joules (J). This is the total hidden energy stored inside a substance—essentially the kinetic energy of all its microscopically vibrating atoms
Energy to change temperature

The Ideal Gas Law 
Before looking at energy transfer, we need to know how gases behave. The Ideal Gas Law links the pressure, volume, and temperature of a trapped gas.
First law of thermodynamics (conservation)
Energy cannot be created or destroyed, only transferred. The change in internal energy of a system equals the heat added to the system minus the work done by the system.

Application: Engine
A heat engine moves heat from hot to cold but uses the heat from the hot source to output useful work to move turbines (e.g. jet engines, steam engines).
Carnot realised the perfect engine would be one that could be run in reverse as a fridge to get back to the same starting point. For fridges, a small temperature difference maximises efficiency (minimal work). The heat engine process of hot → cold cannot be fully reversed without input work. Carnot showed that:
For heat engines, the maximum efficiency depends only on the temperatures.
For heat pumps, the maximum coefficient of performance (COP) also depends only on the temperatures.

For a heat engine:
η=dW/(dQ_1 )=(dQ_1-dQ_2)/(dQ_1 )=1-T_2/T_1 
For a heat pump:
COP=T_1/(T_1-T_2 )
In practice, the real COP never reaches this ideal because of losses in heat exchangers, compressors, etc.

## Quantum physics
Atomic theory in 1820
Quantum physics is the study of the universe at the smallest possible scale—atoms, electrons, and photons. At this level, nature stops acting like predictable, continuous billard balls and starts acting like fuzzy, overlapping waves of probability.
Quantization: Energy is not a smooth ramp; it is a staircase. Matter and light can only absorb or release energy in tiny, discrete packets called quanta.
Wave-Particle Duality: Everything travels like a wave but interacts like a particle. An electron can ripple through space like water, but hits a detector at one precise point.
The Observer Effect: You cannot measure a quantum system without physically disturbing it. Particles do not have definite positions until the moment you look at them
