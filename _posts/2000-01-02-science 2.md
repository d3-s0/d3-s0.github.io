## Astronomy
<img src="/imgs/science/astronomy.PNG" alt="Migration" style="width: 100%; height: auto; display: block">
- Astronomy is studying space using observation and theory. 
- The first physicists were astronomers looking at the stars.
- The Egyptians and Babylonians. Constellations were mapped by Babylonians around 1900BCE. 
- The Greeks then took it and adapted it. 
- The Mayans developed calendars to calculate orbital periods and year lengths more precisely than Europeans. 
- The sky was mapped by them in the study of astrology – this didn’t die until 1600s.  
- Geo-centrism (earth is at the center) was the common conception at the time. Copernicus put forward Heliocentric that sun is at the centre. 
- Galileo arrested for supporting heliocentric.

Big bang theory. How do we know this? There are three main pieces of evidence:
1. Cosmic Microwave background Radiation (CMR): the original light stretched over time shows what was there before. Light got stretched out into longer, cooler wavelengths - microwave radiation.
2. Hubble’s law: galaxies recede from each other. Galactic redshifts (light shifting toward longer, redder wavelengths), the law proved the universe is not static. Means if we go backwards universe was smaller dense spot.
3. Light element abundance. The universe started with only light elements (mostly hydrogen 75% and helium 25%) then nuclear fusion in stars formed heavier elements.
(more on the evidence to be explained in physics post)

- Evidence universe is expanding. 
- Scientists use the doppler shift of light waves to determine whether stars and galaxies are moving toward or away from us. 
- If star is moving towards us, its light wavelength are compressed,, we perceive as slightly bluer. 
- If moving away, as most objects in the universe are, it appears redder.

- Energy from Sun was so intense it pushed lighter elements away. Hence, inner planets have more heavy elements.

- Gravity is the driving force behind the Sun. 
- Gravity draws nebulas close together, creating heat that leads to fusion and star formation. 

- Earth's movements and iron core give it a magnetic field. 
- A compass is simply a magnet that points towards the earth's magnetic north. Earth's motion: Earth spins around an imaginary vertical axis - rotation. One rotation is every 24 hours. Earth also revolves around the Sun - one revolution is 1 year. Earth is at a tilt - 23.44 degrees. Because of the tilt light strikes the surface of earth at different angles at different stages. Earth's orbit + tilt, creates seasons. When the northern hemisphere is tilted towards sun, the sun's rays hits at a higher angle and for longer periods, thus more sun hits. During summer, temperature gets warmer because days get longer and sunlight is more intense at that angle.
When northern hemisphere is tilted form the sun, the suns rays strike at a lower angle and for few hours - this is winter. 
When the northern hemisphere is tilted towards the sun - summer and vice versa.

- Solstices - days that Earth is most tilted towards the sun. The sun reaches the greatest distance north or south of the equator, so the sun is at its highest or lowest in the sky at noon.  Happens around June and December - longest and shortest days. Sun is highest in summer and lowest in winter. 

- Equinox - when earth is not tilted towards or away from sun - happens in between the solicties. 

- The Moon - completes revolution around earth every 27 days. Moon rotates and revolves at the same speed we see same face all the time. Moon has 8 phases it goes through. It glows at night because it reflects the sunlight: new moon, crescent, first quarter, givius, full, gibbous, third quarter, crescent. The moon waxes as it grows and wanes as it shrinks. A lunar cycle is 29.5 days - how long it takes to complete all 8 phases. Moon's tides: gravity pulls the moon but moons gravity also pulls earth -> causes tides which are regular rise and fall of ocean water levels. the high tide point moves across earth as planet rotates under the bulges. usually 6 hours between high tide and low tide.

- Solar eclipse: when moon between earth and sun - moon block light from sun casting a shadow. Lunar eclipse: Earth blocks sunlight hitting moon. Some light goes through earth's atmosphere making moon glow red

- Constellations: shapes and picture people see at night sky are called constellations. Travelers used to use them to navigate. Polaris - north star, right above north pole.

### Magnetism
- Magnetism is a physical force made by moving electrical charges that allow them to attract or repel each other. 
- If you wave a magnet near a coil of wire, the changing magnetic field forces the electrons in the wire to move, creating a voltage

## Thermodynamics
<img src="/imgs/science/thermo.PNG" alt="Migration" style="width: 100%; height: auto; display: block">
- Thermodynamics is the study of heat, work, and energy. 
- It explains how energy moves and changes forms.  
- System & Surroundings: The system is the specific object or space you are studying (like a piston full of gas, or a boiling kettle). The surroundings are everything else outside of it.
- Heat (Q): Measured in Joules (J). This is thermal energy transferring between things due to a temperature difference. 
- Heat always flows naturally from hot to cold. 
- Work (W): Measured in Joules (J). This is mechanical energy transferred when a force moves an object. 
- In thermodynamics, it usually means a gas expanding and pushing a piston. Internal Energy (U): Measured in Joules (J). 
- This is the total hidden energy stored inside a substance—essentially the kinetic energy of all its microscopically vibrating atoms
- Energy to change temperature

## Relativity
<img src="/imgs/science/relativity.PNG" alt="Migration" style="width: 100%; height: auto; display: block">
#### Special relativity
Special relativity is the rule that the speed of light never changes, which means that space and time must stretch or shrink depending on how fast you are moving

#### General relativity (mass and gravity)
General relativity is the rule that mass and energy warp space and time, which is what creates the effect we feel as gravity.

Instead of gravity being an invisible pulling force, imagine space and time as a giant trampoline. A heavy object like the Earth makes a dip in the fabric, and smaller objects like the Moon or a falling apple just roll down that dip.

## Quantum physics
Quantum physics is the study of the universe at the smallest possible scale—atoms, electrons, and photons. At this level, nature stops acting like predictable, continuous billard balls and starts acting like fuzzy, overlapping waves of probability.

Quantisation: Energy is not a smooth ramp; it is a staircase. Matter and light can only absorb or release energy in tiny, discrete packets called quanta.

Wave-Particle Duality: Everything travels like a wave but interacts like a particle. An electron can ripple through space like water, but hits a detector at one precise point.
