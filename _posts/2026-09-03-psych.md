* [Communication](#communication)

Your cerebrum handles thinking, memory, speech, movement, and your five senses.
The cerebral cortex is divided into four main lobes. The Frontal Lobe (pre-frontal cortex), located at the front of the brain. It controls higher-level thinking, including decision-making, planning, problem-solving, and personality. It also contains the motor cortex, which manages voluntary movement and speech. The hippocampus (located inside the temporal lobe) is the engine for learning new information.

The First Attempt: Your frontal lobe focuses intensely on the task, working hard to figure out the steps. Meanwhile, your hippocampus starts recording the new data.
As you practice, electric signals travel between these areas. The more you repeat the action, the thicker the insulation (myelin) around these pathways becomes, making the signal travel faster.
Eventually, the pathway becomes so strong that the skill moves into your subconscious. Your frontal lobe is freed up to focus on the next intelligent choice, and the skill becomes "second nature."

Robert keegan's theory of adult development:
- Stage 1: Impulsive Mind (Early Childhood): Driven by impulses and perceptions.
- Stage 2: Imperial Mind (Adolescence/Some Adults, ~6%): Ruled by personal needs,
wishes, and interests; and driven by "self-sovereign" perspectives.
- Stage 3: Socialised Mind (~58% of adults): wants to be part of the tribe. Able to
think abstractly and of others.
- Stage 4: Self-Authoring Mind (~35% of adults): Individuals can step back from social
expectations to create their own internal authority, values, and sense of identity.
- Stage 5: Self-Transforming Mind (~1% of adults): Individuals can hold multiple,
conflicting systems of meaning, recognizing the limitations of their own perspectives

## Speaking with clarity

Know what you want to say before you open your mouth, then make sure you can be understood.
- concentrate on your consonants to make sure you communicate what you mean
Whajado today?""What did you do today?"The tongue taps the roof of the mouth sharply for each D."I wanna go to the store.""I want to go to the store."The crisp NT and T provide rhythmic punctuation."Gimme a sec.""Give me a second."The biting V and final ND blend prevent slurring.

- highlight key words
- speak slowly enough to be understood
- lacing your sentences with appropriate words 
- avoid overloading your message with too many points



## Putting energy into your voice

Listening with willingness
- 'Listening is the willingness to change'
- Powerful people need to be put in environments where they aren't as powerful to change

Letting go of negatvitiy


Feeding back what you heard
1. Paraphrase what you hear
2. Reflect the feeling behind the message
3. Summarise the speaker's main ideas and feelings to show understanding






Treat the other person with respect, politeness and kindness. How do you do this?
- encourage people to express their ideas and opinions
- letting others finish making their points before interjecting yours
- never insult, disparage or put down people or their points of view
- praise more frequently than you critise, and when you do criticise, focusing o the behaviour and not the person
- treat people the way they want to be treated

Preferred form of communication
- some people like to talk while they think and others prefer silence as they organise their thoughts
- some people like to focus on fine details and practical applications, others prefer concepts
- some people like the written word while others prefer phone or face to face

Thinking before you speak
- speak only if your words are: accurate, appropriate, effective, necessary, timely

Talking less and listening more
- 2 ears and 1 mouth
- confirm understanding by rephrasing what soemone says, and avoid interuptions.

Asking questions to understand
- be interested in what they say
- ask questions to gain information - the more you ask the more you find out

Handling disagreements with diplomacy
- come from different points of view
- handled well, conflict can clear the air and revitalise a relationship
- how to fix?
    - reflect your understanding of the other person's view or opinion. What I am hearing is ...
    - you respect the other person's opinion: I understand how you feel...
    - State your position or opinion - you value their relationship not creating conflict

Opening yourself to new ideas
You have been raised with a set of beliefs and values. The same is true for others. That doesn't mean you are right and they're wrong; you're just different. Open yourself to other points of view:
- make more informed decisions
- increase your personal creativity
- develop certainty, clarity on your own positions

Unconsidered idea lacks justification. When you consider new ides you may discover solid reasons for believing in what you do. Think from another point of view.
