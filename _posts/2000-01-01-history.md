# The big bang
Hello! How did it all begin? It's a question we have all thought at one point or another. The latest scientific theory is the Big Bang theory. The theory states that the universe started approximately 13.8 billion years ago, in one moment where matter was concentrated in a single dense point. Then an explosion caused all matter to expand rapidly. How do we know this? There are three main pieces of evidence:
1. Cosmic Microwave background Radiation (CMR): the original light stretched over time shows what was there before. Light got stretched out into longer, cooler wavelengths - microwave radiation.
2. Hubble’s law: galaxies recede from each other. Galactic redshifts (light shifting toward longer, redder wavelengths), the law proved the universe is not static. Means if we go backwards univese was smaller dense spot.
3. Light element abundance. The universe started with only light elements (mostly hydrogen 75% and helium 25%) then nuclear fusion in stars formed heavier elements.
(more on the evidence to be explained in physics post)

As matter moved through the universe, collisions caused matter to coalesce and form stars. Young stars use hydrogen and helium and release energy through nuclear fusion, held together by their own gravity.

There are approx. 400 billion galaxies in the observable universe, the Milky Way is just one of them!  In the Milky Way, our Sun is a star that formed about 9 billion years ago.

# the earth

The Earth, formed 4.6 billion years ago from dust surrounding the Sun. Then 4.5 billion years ago a Mars sized body collided with Earth, resulting in debris that eventually formed the Moon. 

Earth was a molten mass, constantly bombarded by space matter where it cooled and formed a thin crust. Water vapor condensed, forming Earth's oceans.  

Heavy metallic elements like iron and nickel sank toward the center, forming a liquid outer core and solid inner core. This iron core generated Earth's vital magnetic field.

Lighter silicate rocks floated to the top. Over hundreds of millions of years, the outermost surface exposed to the cold of space cooled down, solidifying into a thin crust

As the thin silicate crust stabilized, it allowed the planet's surface temperature to drop below 100C. The massive volume of water vapor trapped in the atmosphere finally condensed into global, torrential rainfall.

Earth was relentlessly pummeled by thousands of large asteroids and comets. These space rocks also delivered a massive supply of organic molecules (like amino acids and carbon compounds) directly into Earth's primordial soup.

Volcanic activity on the ocean floor created dark, mineral rich underwater vents known as hydrothermal vents.

# first life
Around 3.8 billion years ago, the very first microbial life appeared on Earth. These tiny organisms likely took shape inside the warm, sheltered pockets of underwater hydrothermal vents or volcanic hot springs. 
- The Rock Sponge: Deep-sea vents formed porous rock towers with millions of tiny holes that trapped raw ingredients so they wouldn't wash away.
- Making the Pieces: Volcanic gases mixed with iron and nickel in the rock walls, chemical reactions that forged the first building blocks of life.
- The Crowd Trap: Temperature differences between the warm vent fluid and freezing ocean water packed these ingredients tightly inside the rock holes.
- The First Code: Crowded together, these pieces linked up into chains of RNA that learned how to copy themselves and store instructions.
- Forming the Bubble: Churning water forced oily molecules to fold into tiny, hollow bubbles (lipid micelles) that trapped the RNA inside, creating the first cell bodies.
- Plugging In: The tiny bubbles used the natural electrical charge between the acidic ocean water and the alkaline vent fluid to power their growth.
- Breaking Free: The bubbles evolved internal engines to make their own energy, allowing them to leave the rocks and float into the open ocean as independent life.
<img src="/imgs/life_origin.png" alt="Earth">


- The Oxygen Revolution (2.4 Billion Years Ago): A new type of bacteria evolved that used sunlight for energy (photosynthesis) and pumped out oxygen. This filled the atmosphere with oxygen, created the ozone layer to block deadly radiation, and caused the first major ice age.
- Complex Cells Emerge (2.0 Billion Years Ago): Simple cells swallowed other small cells instead of eating them. Instead of digesting them, they worked together. The swallowed cells became internal engines (mitochondria), creating complex cells called eukaryotes.
- Multi-Cell Life (1.5 Billion Years Ago): Individual complex cells started sticking together to form colonies. Over time, these cells shared jobs (like defense, movement, or eating), creating the very first multicellular organisms, like simple algae.
-  The Cambrian Explosion (540 Million Years Ago): After a series of extreme global ice ages ("Snowball Earth"), life suddenly exploded in variety in the oceans. In a short geological window, creatures evolved hard shells, eyes, skeletons, and teeth. The ancestors of all modern animals appeared here.
- Moving onto Land (475–400 Million Years Ago): Plants moved onto bare rock first, creating soil. Simple bugs followed, and eventually, bony fish evolved lungs and sturdy fins to crawl out of the water, becoming the first four-legged land animals (amphibians).
- The Invention of the Shell Egg (312 Million Years Ago): Amphibians needed to lay eggs in water. A group of animals evolved the amniotic egg—an egg with a hard, protective shell that trapped water inside. This allowed animals to live deep inland, completely free from the ocean, splitting into early reptiles and mammal ancestors.
- The Great Dying (252 Million Years Ago): Massive volcanic eruptions in modern-day Siberia caused catastrophic global warming, wiping out 95% of all ocean life and 70% of land life. This empty, ruined planet left a clean slate.
- The Rise of Dinosaurs and Mammals (230 Million Years Ago): From the few survivors of the Great Dying, two competing groups evolved onto the newly emptied stage: dinosaurs (specialized reptiles) and mammals (warm-blooded, fur-covered survivors).

- The supercontinent Pangea began breaking up into the North America & Eurasia (Europe/Asia) and South America, Africa, India, Antarctica, & Australia.
<img src="/imgs/continent_break.png" alt="Earth">

- Then 66 million years ago, an astroid impact led to end of the age of dinosaurs. 
- The Mammal Takeover (65 to 55 Million Years Ago): With the large dinosaurs gone, the world was left empty. Surviving mammals rapidly diversified into the open ecological spaces, growing larger and splitting into the ancestors of modern horses, whales, and bats
- 7 million years ago, the first hominins emerged with key features being bipedalism and smaller canine teeth. 
    - Bipedalism (Walking on Two Legs): Due to climate change drying out African forests into open grasslands, our ancestors stood up. Walking on two legs was incredibly energy-efficient for long distances.
    - The Hands-Free Bonus: Crucially, walking upright freed our hands. Hands were no longer needed for locomotion, allowing them to evolve incredible dexterity, perfect for gripping, carrying, and eventually, making tools.
- 2.8 million years ago. This is when the genus Homo (our direct biological family) emerged, starting with species like Homo habilis ("Handy Man") and later Homo erectus.
    - The Stone Tool Toolkit: Humans began standardizing tool production. We weren't just picking up rocks; we were planning, shaping, and carrying tools with us.
    - The Cooking Revolution (Around 1.5–2 million years ago):  it unlocked massive caloric energy, causing our ancestors' guts to shrink and their brains to explode in size.

- Then 300,000 years ago, the first Homo sapiens appeared in Africa. 
- 100,000 years ago, Homo sapiens began migrating out of Africa. We started using fire, forming shelters, and hunting with weapons.

# the end of humanity
All good things must come to an End and so will humanity. The question is when and how? The two main causes are by nature and man:

### Man caused
- Climate change - while it's unlikely to cause the end of humanity. It likely will be a cause for the end of modern civilisation. The luxuries we are used to be strained and humanity will have dark periods.

- Engineered pandemics - another COVID like virus could potentially wipe out a large chunk of humanity if we can't find a vaccine.

- Nuclear war - Currently the world is in a mexican stand off, where if one sets of a nuclear bomb so will the others. The danger is if we have one nuke trigger friendly president it’s game over.

- Cyberattacks - security systems could be hacked by rapidly changing technologies like AI causing destabilisation of modern digital infrastructure. 

### Nature caused
- Asteroid impacts (like 66 million years ago when an astroid wiped out the dinosaurs)  

- Sun becomes a red giant: the Sun will engulf Mercury and Venus. Earth may survive but become uninhabitable due to extreme heat.
