# The big bang
How did it all begin? It's a question we have all thought at one point or another. The latest scientific theory is the Big Bang theory. The theory states that the universe started approximately 13.8 billion years ago, in one moment where matter was concentrated in a single dense point. Then an explosion caused all matter to expand rapidly.

As matter moved through the universe, collisions caused matter to coalesce and form stars. Young stars use hydrogen and helium and release energy through nuclear fusion, held together by their own gravity.

There are approx. 400 billion galaxies in the observable universe, the Milky Way is just one of them!  Our solar system began 4.6 billion years ago as a nebula - a floating cloud of gas, ice and dust. A shockwave, probably from a nearby exploding star, caused the nebula to start rotating and condensing. As it rotated, it flattened into a disc shape, like a ball of dough spinning into a pizza crust in the air. Gravity pulled the bits of gas, dust and ice into larger clumps of matter, which, in turn, pulled in more bits of gas, dust and ice. The temperatures and pressure of all the matter at the center became so hot that fusion started and a star, our sun was born. The rest of gas, ice and ust of nebula pulled together into clumps to form planets and moons.

# the earth

The Earth, formed 4.6 billion years ago from dust surrounding the Sun. Then 4.5 billion years ago a Mars sized body collided with Earth, resulting in debris that eventually formed the Moon. 

Earth was a molten mass, constantly bombarded by space matter where it cooled and formed a thin crust. Water vapor condensed, forming Earth's oceans.  

Heavy metallic elements like iron and nickel sank toward the center, forming a liquid outer core and solid inner core. This iron core generated Earth's vital magnetic field.

Lighter silicate rocks floated to the top. Over hundreds of millions of years, the outermost surface exposed to the cold of space cooled down, solidifying into a thin crust

As the thin silicate crust stabilized, it allowed the planet's surface temperature to drop below 100C. The massive volume of water vapor trapped in the atmosphere finally condensed into global, torrential rainfall.

Earth was relentlessly pummeled by thousands of large asteroids and comets. These space rocks also delivered a massive supply of organic molecules (like amino acids and carbon compounds) directly into Earth's primordial soup.

Volcanic activity on the ocean floor created dark, mineral rich underwater vents known as hydrothermal vents.

# first life
<img src="/imgs/history/life_first.png" alt="Earth" style="width: 100%; height: auto; display: block">
- Microbial Origin (3.8B Years Ago): First life forms emerge in warm, protected deep-sea vents.
- The Rock Sponge: Porous underwater rocks trap chemical ingredients in tiny holes.
- Making the Pieces: Volcanic gases react with rock metals to create life's building blocks.
- The Crowd Trap: Temperature shifts compress ingredients tightly inside rock pores.
- The First Code: Crowded chemicals link into self-replicating RNA chains.
- Forming the Bubble: Oily molecules wrap around RNA to create the first cell bodies.
- Plugging In: Cells use natural vent electricity to power their growth.
- Breaking Free: Cells develop internal engines and float out into the open ocean.
- Oxygen Revolution (2.4B Years Ago): Photosynthetic bacteria create oxygen, forming the ozone layer and freezing Earth.
- Complex Cells (2.0B Years Ago): Simple cells swallow others to form mitochondria, creating complex eukaryotes.
- Multi-Cell Life (1.5B Years Ago): Cells cluster into colonies and share jobs, forming simple algae.
- Cambrian Explosion (540M Years Ago): Ocean life diversifies rapidly, developing eyes, shells, skeletons, and animal ancestors.
- Moving onto Land (475–400M Years Ago): Plants create soil, followed by bugs and fish that crawl ashore.
- The Shell Egg (312M Years Ago): Hard-shelled eggs let land animals breed away from water, splitting reptiles and mammals.
- The Great Dying (252M Years Ago): Volcanic global warming wipes out most life, creating a clean slate.
- Dinosaurs & Mammals (230M Years Ago): Reptiles and warm-blooded survivors emerge to dominate the empty planet.

- The supercontinent Pangea began breaking up into the North America & Eurasia (Europe/Asia) and South America, Africa, India, Antarctica, & Australia.
<img src="/imgs/continent_break.png" alt="Earth" style="width: 100%; height: auto; display: block">

- Then 66 million years ago, an astroid impact led to end of the age of dinosaurs. 
- The Mammal Takeover (65 to 55 Million Years Ago): With the large dinosaurs gone, the world was left empty. Surviving mammals rapidly diversified into the open ecological spaces, growing larger and splitting into the ancestors of modern horses, whales, and bats
- 7 million years ago, the first hominins emerged with key features being bipedalism and smaller canine teeth. 
    - Bipedalism (Walking on Two Legs): Due to climate change drying out African forests into open grasslands, our ancestors stood up. Walking on two legs was incredibly energy-efficient for long distances.
    - The Hands-Free Bonus: Crucially, walking upright freed our hands. Hands were no longer needed for locomotion, allowing them to evolve incredible dexterity, perfect for gripping, carrying, and eventually, making tools.
- 2.8 million years ago. This is when the genus Homo (our direct biological family) emerged, starting with species like Homo habilis ("Handy Man") and later Homo erectus.
    - The Stone Tool Toolkit: Humans began standardizing tool production. We weren't just picking up rocks; we were planning, shaping, and carrying tools with us.
    - The Cooking Revolution (Around 1.5–2 million years ago):  it unlocked massive caloric energy, causing our ancestors' guts to shrink and their brains to explode in size.

- Then 300,000 years ago, the first Homo sapiens appeared in Africa. 
- 100,000 years ago, Homo sapiens began migrating out of Africa. We started using fire, forming shelters, and hunting with weapons. 

What made us different:
- Brain: High frontal-lobe connectivity; optimised for abstract thought and speech.
- Specific Comms: Fully flexible, syntax-based language. Can discuss past, future, and invisible concepts
- Knowledge accumulation: we passed on knowledge.
- Social group size: Unlimited group sizes bound together by shared myths and culture (Dunbar's Number ~150 baseline, scaled to millions)
