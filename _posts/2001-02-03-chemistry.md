Chemistry is the study of what stuff is made of.
Common elements are:
- Hydrogen (H): The lightest and most abundant element in the universe; fundamental in nuclear fusion (stars), quantum mechanics (the Bohr model of the hydrogen atom), and astrophysics. Makes up about 74% of all ordinary matter by mass.
- Helium (He): The second-lightest element; critical in thermodynamics, low-temperature physics (liquid helium as a coolant/superfluid), and stellar nucleosynthesis. Accounts for roughly 24% of all ordinary matter by mass
- Carbon (C): Essential for mass-energy equivalence, carbon-14 dating in nuclear physics, and solid-state physics (graphene, graphite, and diamond structures).
- Nitrogen (N) & Oxygen (O): The primary components of Earth's atmosphere, frequently used in fluid dynamics, gas laws (ideal gas equations), and thermodynamics problems.
- Sodium (Na) & Potassium (K): Alkali metals used in atomic spectra, photoelectric effect demonstrations, and conduction studies.
- Copper (Cu), Aluminum (Al), & Silver (Ag): Key electrical conductors used extensively in electromagnetism, circuit analysis, resistance, and Lenz's/Faraday's law problems.
- Iron (Fe), Nickel (Ni), & Cobalt (Co): Ferromagnetic elements central to magnetism, magnetic domains, and electromagnetism.
- Silicon (Si) & Germanium (Ge): Semiconductors that form the foundation of solid-state physics, electronics, diodes, and transistor mechanics.
- Uranium (U) & Plutonium (Pu): Heavy radioactive elements crucial for nuclear physics, fission reactions, binding energy, and radioactive decay chains.

Main Groups on the Periodic Table (Columns 1–18)
- Group 1: Alkali Metals (e.g., Lithium, Sodium, Potassium)
 - Properties: 1 valence electron, soft, silvery, extremely reactive (especially with water), and reactivity increases as you go down the - Group.
- Group 2: Alkaline Earth Metals (e.g., Magnesium, Calcium)
 - Properties: 2 valence electrons, harder and denser than alkali metals, and moderately reactive.
- Groups 3–12: Transition Metals (e.g., Iron, Copper, Gold)
 - Properties: Hard, shiny, ductile, malleable, high melting/boiling points, good electrical conductors, and form colored compounds with multiple oxidation states.
- Group 13: Boron - Group (e.g., Boron, Aluminium)
 - Properties: 3 valence electrons; includes a metalloid (boron) and useful post-transition metals like Aluminium (lightweight, corrosion-resistant).
- Group 14: Carbon - Group (e.g., Carbon, Silicon, Tin, Lead)
 - Properties: 4 valence electrons; ranges from non-metallic carbon to metalloid silicon (semiconductor) and heavy metals like tin and lead.
- Group 15: Nitrogen - Group / Pnictogens (e.g., Nitrogen, Phosphorus)
 - Properties: 5 valence electrons; varied behavior from reactive non-metals to metalloids and metals.
- Group 16: Oxygen - Group / Chalcogens (e.g., Oxygen, Sulfur)
 - Properties: 6 valence electrons; reactive non-metals and metalloids that typically form 2- ions.
- Group 17: Halogens (e.g., Fluorine, Chlorine, Bromine)
 - Properties: 7 valence electrons, extremely high electronegativity, very reactive non-metals that form salts with metals, and reactivity decreases as you go down the - Group.
- Group 18: Noble Gases (e.g., Helium, Neon, Argon)
 - Properties: Full outer valence shells (2 for helium, 8 for others), colorless, odorless, and virtually inert/unreactive under standard conditions.
