Chemistry is the study of what stuff is made of.
Common elements are:
- Hydrogen (H): The lightest and most abundant element in the universe; fundamental in nuclear fusion (stars), quantum mechanics (the Bohr model of the hydrogen atom), and astrophysics. Makes up about 74% of all ordinary matter by mass.
- Helium (He): The second-lightest element; critical in thermodynamics, low-temperature physics (liquid helium as a coolant/superfluid), and stellar nucleosynthesis. Accounts for roughly 24% of all ordinary matter by mass
- Carbon (C): Essential for mass-energy equivalence, carbon-14 dating in nuclear physics, and solid-state physics (graphene, graphite, and diamond structures).
- Nitrogen (N) & Oxygen (O): The primary components of Earth's atmosphere, frequently used in fluid dynamics, gas laws (ideal gas equations), and thermodynamics problems.
- Sodium (Na) & Potassium (K): Alkali metals used in atomic spectra, photoelectric effect demonstrations, and conduction studies.
- Copper (Cu), Aluminum (Al), & Silver (Ag): Key electrical conductors used extensively in electromagnetism, circuit analysis, resistance, and Lenz's/Faraday's law problems.
- Iron (Fe), Nickel (Ni), & Cobalt (Co): Ferromagnetic elements central to magnetism, magnetic domains, and electromagnetism.
- Silicon (Si) & Germanium (Ge): Semiconductors that form the foundation of solid-state physics, electronics, diodes, and transistor mechanics.
- Uranium (U) & Plutonium (Pu): Heavy radioactive elements crucial for nuclear physics, fission reactions, binding energy, and radioactive decay chains
